# Disease Signs

## White spot (ich)
Small white salt-like dots on the body and fins, with fish flicking against decorations. Raise the temperature gradually to 30 C (86 F) for ten days if the species tolerates it, and treat with an ich medication containing malachite green or an alternative labelled for scaleless fish when corydoras are present. Keep treating for three days after the last spot disappears.

## Fin rot
Ragged, shrinking or milky fin edges, usually after poor water quality. Test the water first; repeated 25 percent water changes every day often stop mild cases. For advanced rot, treat with a broad-spectrum antibacterial in a hospital tank and never combine two medications.

## Swim bladder trouble
A fish floating sideways, sinking or struggling to stay level. Fast it for 48 hours, then feed a cooked, peeled pea, and keep the temperature steady near 26 C. Fancy goldfish and bettas are most prone, especially when fed floating flakes that make them gulp air.

## Velvet and fungus
Velvet looks like fine gold dust on the body, best seen with a torch in a dark room; it spreads quickly and needs copper-free treatment in a darkened tank. Cottony white tufts on the skin are fungus, often secondary to injury. Salt at one level teaspoon per 4 liters in a hospital tank helps, except for scaleless fish like corydoras, which need half strength.

## When to act
Gasping at the surface, clamped fins, hiding and refusing food for two days are early warnings. Test ammonia, nitrite and temperature before reaching for medication, because water quality is the cause of most illness.

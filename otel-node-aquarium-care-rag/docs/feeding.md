# Feeding

## How much and how often
Feed adult tropical fish once or twice a day, only as much as they finish in two minutes. Uneaten food rots and spikes ammonia. Healthy adults can skip one feeding day per week; this also gives the filter time to catch up. Fry and juveniles need three small meals a day.

## Choosing foods
A good flake or micro-pellet is the staple. Offer frozen or live bloodworm, daphnia or brine shrimp two or three times a week for color and conditioning. Bottom feeders like corydoras need sinking wafers; drop them in after the lights go out so the faster fish do not take them. Herbivores such as otocinclus need blanched zucchini or algae wafers, not just flakes.

## Holidays
Healthy adult fish can go seven days without food, so a week away does not require a feeding block. Avoid automatic feeders you have not tested for a week beforehand, and do not use vacation blocks, which dissolve slowly and foul the water. For trips over ten days, ask a friend to give one pinch of food every second day.

## Overfeeding signs
Cloudy water, a white film on the surface, fat fish with stringy pale feces and a sudden ammonia reading all point to overfeeding. Cut portions by half and do a 25 percent water change.

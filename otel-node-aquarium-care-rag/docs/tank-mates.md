# Tank Mates

## Peaceful community
Neon tetras, ember tetras, harlequin rasboras, corydoras catfish and otocinclus make a calm group. Keep schooling fish in groups of at least six; a lone tetra turns timid or starts nipping. Corydoras should be kept in groups of four or more on a smooth sand or fine gravel floor.

## Fish to keep apart
Betta splendens males will attack other bettas and often harass long-finned guppies; keep a betta with bottom dwellers and small schooling fish, never with fin nippers. Tiger barbs nip trailing fins, so do not house them with bettas, angelfish or fancy guppies. Large cichlids such as oscars will eat anything that fits in their mouth, including neon tetras.

## Stocking level
As a rule of thumb, allow one liter of water per centimeter of adult fish for small species, and more for messy or active ones. A 60 liter tank comfortably holds a school of eight ember tetras, four corydoras and one dwarf gourami, but not a pair of angelfish, which need at least 150 liters when grown.

## Introducing new fish
Quarantine newcomers for two weeks in a bare tank with a sponge filter. Float the bag for 15 minutes, then add a cup of tank water every five minutes before netting the fish across. Never pour the shop water into your tank.
